# Mathematical sources

- Z. Hashin and S. Shtrikman, “A variational approach to the theory of the elastic behaviour of multiphase materials”, Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids 11 (1963), 127 to 140. The separate elastic upper bounds used for normalisation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5096(63)90060-7).
- G. W. Milton, *The Theory of Composites*, Cambridge University Press (2002), chapter 9. Exact lamination through partial inversion. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511613357).
- G. W. Milton, M. Briane and D. Harutyunyan, “On the possible effective elasticity tensors of 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional printed materials”. Sequential laminates, trace bounds and the void limit. [arXiv:1606.03305](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03305).
- G. W. Milton, “Some open problems in the theory of composites”, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A (2021). Problems 8 to 10, 13 and 14 place the zero-bulk, high-shear question in the wider closure problem. The present results concern fixed solid fractions and do not settle those questions. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2020.0115), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03394).

## Literature search

A literature search completed on 30 October 2026 found no published construction approaching the zero-bulk, full-HS-shear corner in three dimensions at fixed solid fraction, and no published general bound cutting it below the HS square. It covered the Semantic Scholar citation list for the open-problem survey, arXiv searches for work by Milton and Shmuel, and keyword searches for zero bulk modulus, dilational composites, and shear bounds for solid and void. The earlier review was dated 28 October 2026; the 30 October search found no additional result in this setting.

This records what those searches found, not an exhaustive absence claim. Fixed solid fraction and three-dimensional isotropic response matter: a construction in two dimensions, a freely varying fraction, or an anisotropic effective tensor answers a different question. The repository narrows the tested corner with explicit, approximately isotropic constructions; it does not settle the general closure problem or claim priority.
